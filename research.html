Research behind Pivot

Pivot is not a medical product, but its design is informed by peer-reviewed research on planning, emotional disclosure, movement, and reflection. Below are scholarly sources and why they matter for product decisions.

Focus: if-then planning and follow-through

Implementation intentions ("if X happens, then I do Y") are linked to better goal attainment. This informs Focus prompts that turn intention into a concrete next action.

  • Gollwitzer PM, Sheeran P. Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes. Advances in Experimental Social Psychology (2006). DOI: 10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1

Vent: expressive writing and emotional processing

Expressive writing studies suggest that structured emotional disclosure can support psychological and physical health outcomes over time.

  • Smyth JM. Written emotional expression: effect sizes, outcome types, and moderating variables. Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology (1998). PubMed: 9489272
  • Frattaroli J. Experimental disclosure and its moderators: A meta-analysis. Psychological Bulletin (2006). DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.132.6.823

Move: walking, activity, and mental health

Pivot's Move flow is grounded in evidence that physical activity and nature exposure are associated with improved mental well-being and lower depression risk.

  • Bratman GN, et al. Nature experience reduces rumination and subgenual prefrontal cortex activation. PNAS (2015). PubMed: 26124129
  • Mammen G, Faulkner G. Physical activity and the prevention of depression: a systematic review of prospective studies. American Journal of Preventive Medicine (2013). PubMed: 24139780
  • Schuch FB, et al. Physical Activity and Incident Depression: A Meta-Analysis of Prospective Cohort Studies. American Journal of Psychiatry (2018). DOI: 10.1176/appi.ajp.2018.17111194 ( PubMed: 29690792 )

How this maps to product decisions

Research shapes interaction patterns in Pivot:

  • Focus: clear commit prompts and low-friction session starts align with implementation-intention research.
  • Vent: structured expression (voice or text) supports reflection rather than passive scrolling.
  • Move: purposeful walking and outdoor prompts support mood regulation and anti-rumination behavior.
  • Journal: weekly review supports self-monitoring and pattern recognition across behaviors.

Evidence is evolving and individual responses vary. Pivot is a wellness reflection app, not clinical diagnosis or treatment.
